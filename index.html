    About

    A drone photo is mapped into five classes by Chameleon, adapted from only 50 labelled photos (FloodNet) or 20 (RescueNet). A router on 4×4-pixel cells then finds the cheapest way from the entry point to each house: truck on dry ground (roads, or yards at 4× cost; never where water is likely), then a boat across water, then the last stretch on foot (never back into a vehicle). Vehicles have a width: a truck needs about 2.5 m between buildings and a boat about 1.6 m, so neither squeezes through the gap between two houses; people on foot can. The labelled map is the same router run on the human labels: the datasets label pixels, not reachability.

    Confidence: the prediction is routed twice, with the normal off-road rule (land where p_wet < 0.3 for FloodNet, 0.2 for RescueNet, each tuned on the dataset's validation photos) and a cautious one (p_wet < 0.1 / 0.15). Houses a truck reaches even on the cautious map are confident (99 % truly truck-reachable on FloodNet test photos); the others are marked "check first" (74 %). Minutes and metres are rough estimates (truck 20 km/h on roads, 5 off-road, boat 6, on foot 3; scale from the cars in the photo).

    FloodNet 50-shot: mIoU .47 on the full 448-image test (PSPNet with 1,445 labels: .85 on these classes). One showcase scene is an honest failure: shallow water read as dry land.

    Besides the 8 showcase scenes you can browse all 200 test photos of the evaluation split (100 FloodNet, 100 RescueNet), not picked by us: an orange count is the houses a truck cannot reach on the prediction, a pink dot marks a photo where prediction and labelled map disagree. "Water probability" shows what the model believes cell by cell. The address bar keeps the scene, house, entry point and view, so a link opens exactly this view.
